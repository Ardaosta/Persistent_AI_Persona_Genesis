# Connecting a service, the generic walkthrough

Use this when the person has said yes to your help with an account that has no
walkthrough of its own in this folder. The shape is the same every time; only
the screens differ.

## Before you start

1. Say what connecting means in one sentence: "You'll sign in to it yourself,
   in your own browser, and after that I can see and change things there, and
   I'll show you every change before it goes anywhere."
2. Find out which of three ways this service can be reached, in this order of
   preference, and verify from the service's own current documentation, never
   from memory:
   - A hosted connector with a browser sign-in (an official MCP server, or a
     connector the harness offers). Best: they type their password on the
     service's own page and you never hold it.
   - The harness's browser tool, with the person signed in to the service in
     their browser. Fine for reading and for changes they watch.
   - An API key or token. Last resort, and only if the person sets it up
     themselves in the service's own settings page and puts it where the tool
     expects; it never passes through the conversation.
3. If none of the three is possible, say so plainly and offer the next best
   thing: you draft, they paste.

## The walkthrough itself

- One step per message. Say what they will see on screen, what to click, and
  what "it worked" looks like. Wait for them to tell you before the next step.
- If a screen does not match what you described, stop and ask what they see;
  do not guess a click.
- Sign-in pages, two-factor codes, and security questions are theirs. Say "this
  part is yours; I'll wait" and mean it.
- After connecting, do one small read-only thing together and show the result,
  so the first experience of the connection is seeing, not changing.
- Then write the connection into the ledger (connected, date) and, if the
  person gives a standing rule for this service, into the capability recipe
  that covers the work.

## After

Nothing changes on the account without their word for that item or a written
standing rule. The "Hands: what stays in theirs" rules in your manual are the
frame for every service, every time.
