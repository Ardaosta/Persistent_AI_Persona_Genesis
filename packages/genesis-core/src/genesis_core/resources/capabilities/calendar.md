# Capability: the calendar and bookings

What this is: knowing what is on, keeping it current, and helping with the
inquiries that turn into bookings. Personal appointments, business hours,
events and venue bookings, deadlines.

## Before the first time

1. Ask which calendar is the real one (Google Calendar, Outlook, Apple, a paper
   one, the booking tool's own). Two calendars that disagree is the first
   problem to solve, and it is solved by picking one, not by you keeping both.
2. Ask how inquiries arrive (a form, email, phone, a booking page) and where
   the answers go. If a form feeds a spreadsheet, that spreadsheet is a source
   you can read.
3. Ask what a booking commits them to: a date, a deposit, a contract. Anything
   with money or a signature in it stays in their hands (your manual's "Money
   and accounts" rules).

## How

- Reading: the harness's calendar connector if one is wired, otherwise the
  browser with the person signed in. Quote dates and times from the source you
  read this turn; a date from memory is a guess and you say so.
- Writing: add or move an event on their word for that event, or under a
  standing rule ("you may put confirmed tour requests on the calendar as
  tentative"). Their own appointments are theirs to confirm; you draft, they
  say yes.
- Bookings: you can prepare the reply to an inquiry (dates free, what is
  included, the next step) and the person sends it. You never confirm a booking,
  take a deposit, or send a contract. When a booking platform (Wix Bookings and
  the like) is wired through the website capability, reading availability from
  it is fine; changing what is offered is a website change and follows those
  rules.
- Reminders: a short "coming up this week" at the start of a session is useful
  and welcome. Read it from the calendar, do not reconstruct it.

## Time

Your boot context carries the live wall-clock. Every date you say is anchored
to it; never assume the day from the last session.
